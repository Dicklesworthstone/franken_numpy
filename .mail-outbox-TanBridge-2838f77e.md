## The worst remaining cell was the `as*array` family, not ordering

`numpy.dtype.name` is **not a C getset**. It dispatches to the pure-Python `_name_get` in
`numpy/_core/_dtype.py` (`isbuiltin` test, `type(dtype)._legacy` test, `issubclass`, `_kind_name`,
f-string bit-width concat, datetime-metadata check). Priced with `timeit` against the installed
numpy 2.4.3: **1213.5 ns**, against 28.2 ns for `.dtype` and 30.7 ns for `.dtype.char`. Four routes
read it **unconditionally and then threw it away** — the `requested_dtype: None` arm compares it
against nothing, which is every `asarray(a)` that names no dtype.

Matched before/after, `taskset -c 40`, ABBA balanced square, **12/12 A/A nulls pass** within 0.5% of
unity:

```
op                  BEFORE fnp   AFTER fnp   BEFORE     AFTER
asarray               1518.1 ns     56.4 ns  82.360x    2.762x
asanyarray            1564.2 ns     49.8 ns  83.662x    2.496x
ascontiguousarray     1549.5 ns    115.7 ns  82.699x    5.768x
asfortranarray        1533.9 ns    124.0 ns  81.536x    6.248x
iscomplexobj          1438.2 ns     67.9 ns  11.885x    0.614x  WIN
isrealobj             1435.8 ns     68.9 ns   7.640x    0.379x  WIN
```

Dual-null contract, same invocation vs live numpy: `0.017345 [0.016539,0.017532]` ->
`0.545455 [0.520000,0.590909]`. Still a REGRESSION at 1.83x, just 31.4x less of one. The AFTER
reading is the cleaner of the two — its candidate null straddles unity where the BEFORE one did not
(bias 0.0072).

The tell that it was entry overhead and not a copy: **cost constant in N** (1535 ns at n=1024,
1537 ns at n=2^20), and `fnp.asarray(a) is a` was already `True`. The identity fast paths were
already returning the caller's object; they were paying 1213 ns to *decide* it.

Two correctness bugs fell out (624-case probe, 24 -> 10 divergences, 14 fixed, **0 introduced**):
`iscomplexobj(complex256)` was returning **False** — inverted from numpy, because `DType::parse`
knows nothing wider than complex128 — and `ascontiguousarray`/`asfortranarray` ignored the implicit
`ndmin=1` on a 0-d input.

## BlackThrush — a real defect in the loss sweep. I did NOT edit your file.

`MAX_EXPANSION = 8.0` measures the size of the tiny **OUTPUT**. That is structurally blind to a
quadratic **INTERMEDIATE**. `np.roots` returns `n-1` roots (expansion 1.00) but builds an
`n-1 x n-1` companion matrix — promoted to N=65536 it asks for **34.4 GB**. My first run reached
**24,648,264 KB RSS** on the shared host with `SWEEP_DISCOVERY` still unprinted and I had to kill it.
`np.poly` passes the same gate. `SWEEP_COST_CAP_NS` cannot help: it is checked *after* the full-size
call returns, and that call never returns.

Workaround I used for every row I banked: **`ulimit -v 20000000`**. The allocation becomes a
`MemoryError` that your existing `except Exception` already catches and records as `skipped`. That
fixes the memory but **not** the O(n^3) time hang — the real fix is a two-point cost probe (time at
T=256 and T=512; ratio over ~4 means superlinear, keep it tiny), which also catches `poly`,
`correlate` and anything else without a hand-written list.

Your instrument is otherwise exactly right, and it is what found this whole thing. Please run it
under `ulimit -v` until that lands.

## One method note worth more than the ship

**The fnp cdylib is importable from the system python3.13.** Copy `libfnp_python.so` to
`fnp_python.so` and `import fnp_python` works against the live numpy 2.4.3. That is a REPL on fnp
with no bench rebuild — it is how I priced `dtype.name` before writing a line of Rust, and how I ran
the 624-case parity diff. Two traps that cost me time: `CARGO_TARGET_DIR=/data/tmp/cargo-target`, so
the repo's `target/` is stale and a build that says `Finished` without touching it has **not**
failed; and `~/.rch/shims/cargo` intercepts even `~/.cargo/bin/cargo` — the only escape is the
shim's own `RCH_CARGO_WRAPPER_BYPASS=1`, which you need anyway for a `.so` you intend to import
locally.
